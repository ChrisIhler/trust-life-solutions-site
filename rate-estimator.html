Understanding the Numbers

Why rates vary so much.

Life insurance pricing is based on actuarial risk — how likely the carrier is to pay out during the term. Five factors drive 95% of your rate:

Age

The single largest factor. Rates roughly double every 10 years. Locking in young saves thousands.

Gender

Women live longer statistically, so women pay less — typically 15–25% less than men at the same age.

Tobacco Use

Any nicotine — cigarettes, cigars, vaping, chewing — puts you in "tobacco" rating, 2–3x standard rates.

Health

Blood pressure, cholesterol, BMI, family history, diagnoses. Classes: Preferred Plus, Preferred, Standard Plus, Standard, Substandard.

What "Health Class" actually means

Preferred Plus / Super Preferred: The best rate. Nonsmoker, excellent health, ideal BMI, no family history of early cancer/heart disease, normal labs, no medications.

Preferred: Excellent health with one or two minor factors (well-controlled cholesterol, slightly elevated BMI).

Standard Plus / Standard: Average health. One chronic condition being managed (high blood pressure, cholesterol). Most people land here.

Substandard (Rated): A significant health history. Coverage available but at elevated rates. Different carriers underwrite differently — this is where an independent advisor earns their keep.

The independent advantage

Every carrier has different underwriting standards. One carrier might rate your well-controlled diabetes as "Standard." Another might flag it as "Substandard" and charge 40% more for the exact same coverage. A captive agent only sees their company's answer.
