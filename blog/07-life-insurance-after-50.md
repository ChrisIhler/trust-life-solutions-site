# Life Insurance After 50: Your Real Options in 2026

**Target keywords:** life insurance over 50, life insurance for seniors, life insurance after 60, burial insurance
**Reading time:** 6 min
**Target audience:** Adults 50+ searching for coverage, often for final expenses or final income gap

---

If you're over 50 and shopping for life insurance, you're dealing with a market that quietly changed in the last decade. Pricing for healthy 50-, 60-, and even 70-year-olds is more competitive than it used to be. No-medical-exam options are widely available. And a handful of products specifically built for the senior market didn't exist 15 years ago.

The catch: the advertising for senior life insurance is aggressive and often misleading. Television commercials promise "$9.95 a month" policies that turn out to have two-year waiting periods and $5,000 death benefits. Direct mail offers are full of fine print. An independent conversation with someone licensed, quoting multiple carriers, is almost always cheaper and more appropriate than what you'll find on your own.

Here's what you actually need to know.

## The three products senior applicants should understand

### 1. Term Life Insurance (for those still working or carrying debt)

If you're 50-65, still earning, still carrying a mortgage, and still have people depending on your income, term life insurance probably still makes sense — and the premiums are far more affordable than most people assume.

Term insurance stops making sense in your late 60s and beyond — the pricing curve steepens, term lengths shorten, and coverage ends before most people think it will. By age 70, most shoppers should be looking at permanent products instead.

### 2. Guaranteed Universal Life (GUL)

A misunderstood product that's often the best choice for the 55-75 age band.

**What it is:** A universal life policy designed to provide a guaranteed death benefit for life (or to age 95, 100, 105, or 121 depending on design) with a level premium. Unlike traditional whole life, it's not designed to build much cash value — the whole point is guaranteed death benefit at the lowest possible cost.

**Why it's often the right answer for 55-75:** Permanent coverage at about 30-50% of the cost of traditional whole life, with a guaranteed death benefit your heirs are certain to receive.

**Rough ballpark:** $250k of GUL to age 100 for a healthy 60-year-old male non-smoker runs about $245/month. Same policy for a 60-year-old female runs about $210/month. Compare to whole life at $420/month or more for similar guarantees.

**Best carriers in 2026:** Pacific Life Discovery GUL, Protective Advantage Choice UL, Nationwide No-Lapse Guarantee UL, Symetra AccumulatorUL.

### 3. Final Expense Whole Life (Burial Insurance)

Small-face whole life policy ($5,000-$30,000) designed specifically for funeral costs and end-of-life expenses. Easy to qualify for — a handful of health questions, no medical exam. Available to most applicants up to age 85.

**When it makes sense:** You want to cover funeral costs (averaging $10,000-$15,000) without leaving the burden to family. You don't have $25,000 sitting in cash earmarked for this. Your health doesn't allow for competitive pricing on traditional term or GUL.

**The three sub-types you'll encounter:**

**Level Benefit (Immediate):** Full death benefit from day 1. Requires you to answer health questions and qualify. Best pricing.

**Graded Benefit:** Lower payout for the first 2-3 years (often return of premiums + 10-20%), full death benefit thereafter. For applicants who can't qualify for Level.

**Guaranteed Issue:** No health questions, guaranteed acceptance. 2-3 year waiting period (return of premium if you die during the waiting period). Highest priced. Only choose this if Graded is also unavailable.

**Rough pricing for $10,000 of level benefit final expense, healthy non-smoker:**

|---|---|---|
| 60 | $38/mo | $29/mo |
| 65 | $49/mo | $37/mo |
| 70 | $66/mo | $51/mo |
| 75 | $98/mo | $75/mo |
| 80 | $148/mo | $115/mo |

**Top carriers:** Mutual of Omaha Living Promise, Americo Eagle Premier, Foresters PlanRight, Royal Neighbors of America, Gerber Life.

## The mistakes senior applicants most commonly make

### Mistake 1 — Buying the first TV advertised policy

You've seen the commercials. "$9.95 a month." "No medical exam." "Accept anyone." Most of these are guaranteed-issue policies with two-year graded death benefits — if you die in the first 24 months, your family gets your premiums back plus 10% interest, not the full coverage amount. Priced 40-80% higher than what an independent agent would quote you for comparable coverage.

If you're healthy, you're subsidizing the high-risk applicants in the same pool. Walk through an independent agent first.

### Mistake 2 — Cancelling group life without a replacement

When you retire, your employer group life coverage ends (or converts to something expensive). Many retirees discover at the exit interview that they can "convert" their group term to a permanent policy — usually at triple the cost of comparable coverage on the open market.

The better move: three months before retirement, apply for an individual policy with a private carrier while you're still healthy and employed. Place the private policy first. Then let the group coverage lapse or decline the conversion offer.

### Mistake 3 — Assuming you can't qualify

Diabetic? Heart condition? Had cancer 8 years ago? Take medications for blood pressure? The coverage market for moderately-impaired senior applicants is vastly better than it was a decade ago. Carriers like Prudential, Mutual of Omaha, and Corebridge write coverage routinely for conditions that would have been auto-declines in 2015.

If you've been told you're uninsurable, get a second opinion from an independent agent. One carrier's "no" is another carrier's "yes."

### Mistake 4 — Buying too much permanent coverage

On the other end, some senior applicants get sold very large whole life policies they don't need. If your goal is to cover final expenses and leave a modest legacy, a $250k GUL or a $25k final expense policy is plenty. You don't need $1M of permanent coverage.

A good advisor will ask you: "What's this money for?" If the honest answer is "funeral costs and maybe a small gift to each grandkid," your budget for coverage is small and targeted. Anything else is oversold.

### Mistake 5 — Not reviewing existing coverage

If you bought life insurance 20-30 years ago, there's a strong chance today's market would give you more for less. This is especially true for cash-value policies that have been minimally managed — many old whole life and universal life policies from the 1990s and early 2000s are under-funded and at risk of lapsing.

A 20-minute policy review with an independent agent costs nothing and often finds either savings or problems (or both) that would have cost thousands over time.

## Health conditions and what they actually mean for pricing

| Condition | Practical pricing impact |
|---|---|
| Well-controlled hypertension | Minimal — often Standard class or better |
| Type 2 diabetes, oral meds only, A1C under 7.0 | Standard class with Prudential, Banner, Mutual of Omaha |
| Type 2 diabetes on insulin | Substandard (Table 2-4), 50-100% higher than Standard |
| Recent cancer (less than 2 years since treatment) | Usually postponed; FE policies available |
| Cancer 5+ years in remission | Often Standard class |
| Heart attack or bypass 3+ years ago | Table 2-4 rated at most carriers |
| COPD or moderate asthma | Standard or Table 2 depending on severity |
| Sleep apnea with CPAP compliance | Standard Plus commonly available |
| Tobacco use in last 12 months | Tobacco rates (2-3× standard) |

## Should you buy now or wait?

If you're over 50 and don't have coverage, the right answer is almost always "now, not later." Every year of delay means:

- Higher premiums (4-7% increase per year on average after 50)
- More health events that can disqualify you
- Fewer carriers willing to quote longer terms
- Less time to age-in to preferable products

The exception: if you're mid-treatment for a significant condition (cancer, cardiac event, major surgery), wait 6-12 months post-recovery and re-apply. Mid-treatment you'll either be declined or massively overcharged; post-stabilization you'll get a much better offer.

## The 20-minute conversation that usually answers it

For anyone over 50 wondering what their real options are, a 20-minute phone call with an independent agent should cover:

1. Your current health profile and any medications
2. Your target coverage amount and purpose (income replacement / legacy / final expenses)
3. A quick pass across 3-5 carriers to identify your best-fit options
4. A ballpark of what you'd actually pay

If the call ends and you don't have concrete numbers to make a decision on, the agent didn't do their job.
